Spaced repetition (SRS) is why you can learn hundreds of characters without drowning in reviews. It's simple in principle: show a card right before you'd forget it. This guide is how to *use* the scheduler in Kaiteyo — grading honestly, trusting the intervals, and keeping the queue healthy.

## The engine

Kaiteyo uses **FSRS-5**, a modern scheduler that adapts per card to your review history — not fixed intervals stamped on every card. Each card carries a state (new, learning, review, relearning), and every answer updates it. The wiki has the full mechanics in [how spaced repetition works](/wiki/spaced-repetition/); this guide is the practice.

## The four grades, used correctly

| Grade | What it means | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Again** | I didn't know it | Actually didn't know it — not "almost" |
| **Hard** | I knew it, barely | Recognised it with effort or a wrong first guess |
| **Good** | I knew it | Knew it in a second or two |
| **Easy** | Too easy | Knew it instantly, and it's been a while |

The most important habit: **grade the card, not your ego**. "Good" on something you half-guessed is how intervals get inflated and cards fall out of memory. "Again" is not a failure — it's the scheduler's most important signal. One missed card reschedules it tightly and costs you nothing.

## New vs review

Keep the new-card intake lower than your appetite, not equal to it. New cards become reviews in a few days; a burst of 50 new cards today becomes 50+ reviews a day next week. A stable rule: **new ≈ half of what you're willing to review daily**. The daily limits in settings exist exactly for this — set a review cap and let the queue sort itself out.

## Trust the intervals

New learners are constantly tempted to "check" cards, reschedule everything, or review ahead of schedule. That fights the entire mechanism. The scheduler computes intervals from *your* history; if you grade honestly, the intervals become right for *you* — longer for what you know, shorter for what you don't. Adjust *strictness* (via grading), not *timing*.

## The bury and suspend tools

- **Bury** — park a card for the rest of the session (useful mid-session for a word you'll see in the next sentence)
- **Suspend** — hide a card you don't want to see for a while (a word you already know perfectly, or one you're not ready for)

These are normal workflow tools. Using them well keeps the queue clean, which keeps you showing up.

## When reviews feel like too much

1. **Check your new-card rate** — this is the cause 90% of the time
2. **Use daily limits** — hard cap on reviews per day
3. **Let "Again" do its job** — a card you keep failing stays in learning state, it doesn't pile up as a giant overdue stack

The scheduler is calibrated to be survivable. If your queue is terrifying, the fix is intake, not abandonment.

## What you're buying

Done right, SRS turns a few minutes a day into permanent retention. The statistics screen shows you the payoff — retention curves, per-deck accuracy, growth over time. It's the most rewarding screen in the app when your habits are good.
