title: Understanding your statistics
description: Every statistic in Kaiteyo answers a question. Here's what the numbers mean and how to use them without obsessing.

Kaiteyo's statistics are computed from your actual review history — no fabricated charts, no numbers that contradict the rest of the app. This guide is what each group of numbers is actually telling you.

## The rules of the game

Every statistic is real, offline, and derived from the same data as Study and the Library. The definitions are documented (see the [statistics documentation](/docs/features/statistics/)); the short version:

- **Studied** — at least one recorded review
- **Learned** — reviewed with an interval of a day or more
- **Mature** — interval of 21+ days
- **Mastered** — interval of 180+ days

Those definitions are shared everywhere, so "learned" on your dashboard and "learned" in a deck report mean the same thing.

## Reading the overview

- **Reviews today / streak** — the habit meter. This is the number to keep *above zero*, not to maximise.
- **Study time** — honesty about effort, separated from leisure watch time in media.
- **Heatmap** — a year of consistency at a glance. Look for *gaps*, not peaks. Consistency is the entire game.

## Retention: the number that matters

**Retention** is the share of reviews you answer correctly over a window — per grade, per interval bucket, per deck. This is the closest thing to a health check:

- **Too high (95%+)** — you might be reviewing material that's too easy. Bump new-card intake or study harder decks.
- **Too low (< 80%)** — you're overreaching or intake is too high. Cut new cards; let intervals catch up.
- **Dropping per deck** — that specific deck needs attention, maybe more writing, maybe a re-order.

Retention per *interval bucket* is the scheduler's own report card: if long intervals have worse retention than the scheduler expects, it re-calibrates.

## Velocity: pace, not ego

Velocity is trailing-30-day rates — reviews per day, new items per week, study hours per week. It answers one question: *am I moving?* Don't compare it to anyone else's; compare it to last month.

## Knowledge coverage

JLPT and frequency coverage shows what fraction of each level's material you've studied. This is the "where am I in the whole system" view — a sanity check when the daily grind feels aimless. N5 coverage creeping up is real progress you can show to yourself.

## Exams as measurement

Exams measure knowledge *differently* from flashcard reviews — no hints, no "knew it eventually", production (writing) and recognition questions from your actually-studied content. The weekly exam is the scheduled one. The **exam score delta** (second half accuracy vs first half) tells you whether you're warming up or fading during a test — a fatigue signal, not a knowledge score.

## Goals

Goals derive from real counters, and every change is appended to goal history — so the "do I complete my goals" trend is computed from snapshots, never reconstructed. Two good defaults: **daily review minimum** and **weekly exam attendance**.

## The anti-obsession clause

Statistics exist to answer questions and correct course — not to be checked hourly. The most useful cadence:

- **Daily**: reviews today (keep it simple)
- **Weekly**: retention + velocity + weekly exam
- **Monthly**: knowledge coverage, JLPT progress, goal completion

If a number makes you *stop* studying, that number is being used wrong. Log out, go watch an episode, and mine a sentence.
