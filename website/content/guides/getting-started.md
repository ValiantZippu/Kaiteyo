Learning Japanese is a long road, and the first month matters more than the first hour. This guide is the on-ramp we'd recommend to a friend: what to learn first, what to install, and how to make the habit stick without burning out.

## What you're actually learning

Japanese has three scripts plus vocabulary and grammar:

- **Hiragana** (ひらがな) — the phonetic script for native words, 46 core characters
- **Katakana** (カタカナ) — the phonetic script for loanwords, another 46
- **Kanji** (漢字) — thousands of logographic characters borrowed from Chinese
- **Vocabulary & grammar** — the actual language

None of this is inherently hard. It's a lot of small pieces, and the trick is a system that keeps the pieces in front of you until they stick. That's exactly what Kaiteyo is built for.

## Start with kana

Before anything else, learn hiragana and katakana. You need them to read anything, and they take a week or two of light daily practice. Create a kana deck in Kaiteyo (the app resolves readings and stroke data automatically), study it with the writing mode enabled, and write every character — recognition is the warm-up, writing is the workout.

A good daily target: **10–15 minutes, every day**. Consistency beats intensity at this stage.

## Add kanji with structure

Once kana feels automatic, start a kanji deck. The bundled JLPT decks (N5 first) are the natural path — they're ordered by how often you'll actually meet the characters.

For each kanji, Kaiteyo shows you readings, meanings, radicals, stroke count and stroke order automatically. The habit to build:

1. **See** it in a card
2. **Write** it — stroke order matters, the app grades every stroke
3. **Meet** it again in vocabulary
4. **Meet** it again in media

You don't need to write every kanji you ever learn. But the ones you write become the ones you *know* — writing is the difference between recognizing a shape and owning it.

## Build vocabulary as you go

Vocabulary is where Japanese starts feeling real. The N5 vocabulary deck pairs with the kanji you're learning — 日本 means *Japan* and you already know 日.

A habit that works: when you learn a kanji, look for a word that uses it, and study that word too. The dictionary is built in, so the lookup takes two seconds instead of breaking your flow.

## The weekly rhythm

Something like this, adjusted to your life:

| Day | What | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Daily | Kana or kanji reviews | 5–10 min |
| Daily | 5 new cards (learn + write) | 5 min |
| Every other day | Vocabulary deck | 10 min |
| Whenever | Media with subtitles — look things up | fun time |

## Don't optimise the first month

Forget study plans, forget "optimum interval multipliers", forget comparing yourself to anyone. The single best predictor of progress is **still doing it tomorrow**. Kaiteyo's scheduler handles the *when*; your job is just to show up.

## When it feels stuck

The "everything is new" phase lasts longer than you expect, and that's normal. Trust the process — spaced repetition quietly converts what feels like noise into recognition.
