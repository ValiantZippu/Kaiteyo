Grammar is where vocabulary learning stalls: you know the words, but the glue that connects them is a blur. The Grammar workspace fixes that with pattern drills — a curated set of real Japanese grammar patterns, each with its structure explained and example sentences drawn from the pattern itself.

## What you get

The grammar index ships with a curated reference deck of common patterns (て-form, 〜ている, 〜たい, 〜から, 〜ので, conditionals, 〜そうです, and friends), each recorded with:

- The **pattern** itself (e.g. 〜ている)
- Its **structure** — what the pieces are
- A **meaning** and **register** note
- Real **example sentences** with the pattern highlighted

Every entry carries provenance, so you know where the fact came from.

## The loop

**1. Open the Grammar workspace.** You get a practice deck derived from the curated facts — each pattern becomes a drill item.

**2. See the pattern, recall the use.** The card shows the pattern and its example. Test yourself on the meaning, the structure, or both.

**3. Let the index back you up.** The same engine powers lookups, so when you meet a pattern in the wild — in the reader, a subtitle, or a browser page — it resolves against the same curated facts.

## Where grammar meets the rest

Because the grammar index is a real searchable index rather than a hardcoded list, it's the natural home for future pattern mining: select a sentence anywhere in the suite and see which patterns it contains. The practice view and the index share one source of truth.

## The honest limits

- The built-in deck is curated and intentionally small — comprehensive grammar datasets are gated by licensing review in the data sources documentation.
- Drills are single-pattern recall; full sentence-level parsing is a larger project.

See the [grammar engine documentation](/docs/features/grammar/) for the index design and the curated facts list.
