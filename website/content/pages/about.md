Kaiteyo (書いてよ — "write it!") is the open-source continuation of [Kanji Dojo](https://github.com/syt0r/Kanji-Dojo), rebuilt in Kotlin Multiplatform with one design language across every platform.

## Why it exists

Most Japanese-learning tools are islands. A flashcard app doesn't know what you looked up in your dictionary. Your dictionary doesn't know what you're mining from anime. Your writing practice doesn't feed your review schedule. Learners end up managing five tools and re-entering the same word in each of them.

Kaiteyo exists to connect those islands. One database, one scheduler, one statistics system — and every workflow feeds into it.

## The philosophy

**Connected.** Every action contributes to the same learning system. Encounter a kanji in media → look it up → mine it → study it → write it → see it in statistics → meet it again in an exam. Nothing is a dead end.

**Independent.** Kaiteyo owns its own decks, cards, scheduler, reviews and statistics. Anki integration is optional and additive, never a dependency.

**Offline-first.** Your study data lives on your device. No account required, no cloud lock-in, no tracking — study anywhere, including on a plane.

**Write, don't just recognize.** Reading kanji is half the battle. Stroke-order writing practice with real feedback is built into the study loop, not bolted on.

**Serious but calm.** The interface is quiet, consistent and fast — OLED black and dark themes, restrained design, keyboard-first on desktop. The complexity lives underneath, where it belongs.

## Open source

Kaiteyo is free and open source under the [GPL-3.0 license](/license/). Its data comes from openly licensed datasets — [KanjiVG](https://kanjivg.tagaini.net/), [KANJIDIC](https://www.edrdg.org/kanjidic/kanjdicindex.html), [JMdict](https://www.edrdg.org/jmdict/j_jmdict.html) and more — with full [attribution](/data/). Read the source on [GitHub](https://github.com/ValiantZippu/Kaiteyo), and join the [community](/community/).
