---
title: How spaced repetition works
description: The science behind why Kaiteyo schedules reviews the way it does.
---

Spaced repetition is a study technique built on a simple observation: if you review something *just before you are about to forget it*, you remember it much longer with far less effort than reviewing it on a fixed schedule.

## The forgetting curve

Shortly after you learn something, your memory of it fades quickly. Each successful recall slows that fading, and the review interval grows with every successful recall: 1 day, then 3, then 7, then 14, then a month…

## In Kaiteyo

Kaiteyo tracks every card individually. When you grade a review:

- **Again** — you forgot; the card comes back soon
- **Hard** — it was difficult; a short interval
- **Good** — you got it; the scheduled interval
- **Easy** — too easy; the interval jumps ahead

The scheduler keeps separate timers per card, so a deck with 500 cards never forces you to review all of them at once — you only see the ones that are due.

## Tips

- **Do reviews daily.** A 10-minute daily session beats a two-hour weekly grind.
- **Trust the scheduler.** Don't skip "Again" to save time — the honest grade is what keeps intervals accurate.
- **Add new cards in small batches** and let the schedule even out before adding more.
