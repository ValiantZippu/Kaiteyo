Learning a kanji is commonly split into two skills:

- **Reading (recognition)** — you see 書 and know it reads しょ/かく and means "to write".
- **Writing (production)** — you can reproduce 書 from memory, in the right stroke order.

Most learners over-practice recognition and under-practice writing. That's why Kaiteyo offers both card modes: recognition cards build reading speed, while writing cards build muscle memory.

## Why stroke order matters

Japanese stroke order is not arbitrary decoration:

- Writing strokes top-to-bottom and left-to-right keeps characters balanced.
- Correct order makes handwritten text legible — strokes are the cues readers use.
- Handwriting input methods and dictionary lookup by radical assume correct order.

Kaiteyo's writing practice grades your strokes as you draw them, so you get instant feedback on direction and order — not just on the final shape.

## A balanced routine

Try alternating modes: one session reading recognition cards, the next writing the same deck. Production difficulty quickly reveals which characters you only *recognize* rather than actually know.
